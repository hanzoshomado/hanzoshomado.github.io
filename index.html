[Game title]

Multiplayer co-op adventure

Overview

A multiplayer co-op game I'm currently developing in Unity with Photon Fusion.

Players land on an island and gather loot before the storm comes, when they fight a final boss.

Combat

Readable attacks are a core component of making combat fair and rewarding.

Animals are carefully designed to telegraph attacks before they land. A glint star flashes before they strike, which gives the player enough time to dodge. Animals behave intelligently, and each one is unique.

Weapon skills affect the environment itself. Ice will freeze fire that's spreading.

First Person Body

The game is in first person. If you look down, you'll see your shadow alongside your hands and feet. Your teammates will see the same player fighting at full size.

Playing together

Up to four players can connect to one world. On the left, the host fights up close with a great sword, while the client on the right covers with a minigun.

One problem I ran into was the held weapon. In first person it's drawn small so it fits your own view, which made it look tiny from a teammate's camera. To check what other players actually see, I test co-op by running two separate builds of the game in one session, about 30 ms apart, and recording both screens at the same moment.

Level design and architecture

Each of the game's zones is carefully planned out. Slope and relief maps help dictate where players can walk and climb. Danger rises as the player goes further from spawn, and animals gate further zones with better loot.

Tetherblade

A 2D action platformer made in GameMaker at NYU Tisch's Future Game Designers program

Overview

TETHERBLADE is a 2D platformer I made with a partner in GameMaker for NYU's Future Game Designers program.

We were given the verb "extend" to work with, so the player has a grappling hook that allows them to traverse up to the final boss, the Wyrmreaper.

As an architecture student, I designed the level as a piece of architecture: a shrine approach that changes as you climb, using space, light and materials to guide the player instead of relying on signs.

Grapple traversal

Zip from perch to perch up a dark shaft, climb out into the open, then swing under a gallery and let go to fly across a gap.

Moth chain

Grappling a moth flies you straight through it with a slash and bounces you off, so you can chain moth to moth across a gap.

Enemies that teach moves

A beetle whose roof-tile shell turns the sword aside, so you flip it with the grapple. A stone guardian whose lobbed embers you cut out of the air with an up-slash.

The Wyrmreaper

The full boss fight: its entrance, readable attack tells, a knockdown, a second phase under a red sky, and its defeat at sunrise.

The climb as architecture

A walk up the level, from a low portico (compression) through a dark shaft lit only by moonlight from above, out to the summit gate against the sun (release). Then a tunnel of torii gates, a quiet plaster hall, and the light well up into the Sanctum.

Hero's Journey

A narrative adventure for English class, made in Unity and C#

Overview

I made this game as a project for an assignment on the Hero's Journey in my English class. I used game design as a medium for storytelling.

Branching dialogue

Whenever dialogue happens, the player is presented with multiple options. These options affect how the story plays out.

Differing Endings

Unlike a traditional novel, a game lets you depict multiple possible endings. The ending you receive depends on the choices you made throughout the game.

Characters and cutscenes

When the player walks up to an NPC, a prompt appears to talk to them. Some conversations start a cutscene made with Unity's Timeline, so important story moments play out on screen instead of only in text.

Player guidance

Players are guided by an objective marker and a minimap. Tutorial hints also pop up in certain spots to help the player.

Cinematic intros

Each world in the game stands for a different stage of the Hero's Journey. When the player enters a new world, a short cutscene introduces the place and what the hero is facing, so the player knows where they are in the story before they start playing.
